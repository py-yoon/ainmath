Look at an object,
and you'll see its shape family

Early elementary · About 3 min read

A shipping box, a soup can, a soccer ball — they all look different, but every one of them belongs to one of three shape families.

🎮 Try the Solid Shape Families activity 📚 See other topics
🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Read this together with a grown-up. Some words may still be tricky to read alone. Reading it out loud together makes it easier to understand.

1There are three shape families

📦 Box-shapedhas flat faces
🥫 Cylinder-shapedhas both curved and flat faces
⚽ Ball-shapedhas no flat faces at all

You'll learn these three families again later at school under their formal names: rectangular prism, cylinder, and sphere.

Tell them apart by checking: any flat faces? curved and flat together? or no flat faces at all?

2Check whether it has a flat face

If touching an object with your hand finds a flat part, it belongs to the box-shaped family — think books, bricks, or dice. Flat faces let it stack up steadily on top of other things.

3Curved and flat together means cylinder-shaped

A soup can or a roll of paper towels is curved on the side but flat on the top and bottom — that's the cylinder-shaped family. Lie it on its side and it rolls; stand it up and it stacks steadily.

4No flat face anywhere means ball-shaped

A soccer ball or an orange feels curved no matter where you touch it — that's the ball-shaped family. With no flat part anywhere, it rolls smoothly in any direction.

You can also tell them apart by how well they roll.
Box-shaped objects barely roll at all, cylinder-shaped objects roll only when lying on their side, and ball-shaped objects roll easily in any direction.

5Try playing like this

  • Sort objects around your house into shape families. Try classifying things like a fridge, a cup, or a piece of candy into one of the three families.
  • Explain why, while touching the object. Try saying the reason out loud, like "because it has a flat face" or "because it's round all over."
  • Test yourself quickly with the quiz. Practice recognizing the shape family right away just from an object's name and picture.
