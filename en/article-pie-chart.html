Divide It Up
Like Slicing a Cake

Upper elementary · About 3 min read

Say you survey your classmates about their favorite season — how do you show the results at a glance? A pie chart is a great way.

🎮 Try Pie Chart Lab 📚 See other topics

1The whole circle is 100%

A pie chart shows all your survey data as a single circle. The whole circle stands for 100%, and each category gets sliced out as a wedge sized to match its share. It's a lot like cutting a single cake into several pieces.

2Percentage = (part) ÷ (whole) × 100

If 8 out of 20 people say summer is their favorite, you find the percentage by dividing 8 by 20 and multiplying by 100.

8 ÷ 20 × 100 = 40%

40% of everyone surveyed prefers summer.

3Central angle = percentage × 3.6°

A full circle is 360 degrees. Since 100% corresponds to 360 degrees, 1% works out to 3.6 degrees (360÷100). So to find any category's central angle, just multiply its percentage by 3.6.

40% × 3.6° = 144°

The wedge representing summer has a central angle of 144 degrees.

The bigger the percentage, the bigger the wedge and its central angle — add them all up and you get 100% and 360°
Percentages always add up to 100%, and central angles always add up to 360°.
On the activity page's table, check that adding up every category's percentage gives 100%, and adding up every central angle gives 360°.

4How is this different from a bar graph?

A bar graph is great for comparing category sizes by bar length, while a pie chart is great for seeing at a glance what share each category takes up of the whole. When you're wondering "what percent of the class does this category make up?", a pie chart is the better fit.

5Try this

  • Increase the count for one category. Check how its wedge, percentage, and central angle all grow together.
  • Make every category the same size. Check that four equal categories each split into 25% and 90°.
  • Try a different survey topic. See that the same idea applies just as well to other data — favorite sport, blood type, anything else.
