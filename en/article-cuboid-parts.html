What You Can See Is
Only Half the Story

Look at a shipping box from the front and you'll only see about 3 faces and 9 edges. But even without flipping the box around, you can work out that the hidden side has just as much on it.

1The three parts of a rectangular prism

A rectangular prism is made up of flat faces, straight edges where two faces meet, and pointed vertices where three edges meet.

2Opposite faces are always the same size

A rectangular prism has three pairs of opposite faces: top-bottom, front-back, and side-side. Looking from one direction, you only see 3 faces, but there's a matching, same-size face on the opposite side of each one — 6 faces in total.

3A cube is a special rectangular prism

A rectangular prism whose length, width, and height are all equal is called a cube. A cube still has 6 faces, 12 edges, and 8 vertices — what makes it special is that all six of those faces are congruent squares.

4You can count parts even when you can't see them

There's a trick for finding the hidden parts.
Any face, edge, or vertex you can't see from the front exists in exactly the same way on the far side. Add the number you can see from the front to the number that's hidden, and you get the exact total.
