The equals sign (=)
means balance, like a scale

Elementary · About 3 min read

In "3+4=7", the equals sign isn't just a marker for "here comes the answer." It means the left and right sides are exactly the same, like a balance scale.

🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Read this together with a grown-up. Some words may still be tricky to read alone. Reading it out loud together makes it easier to understand.

1The equals sign doesn't mean "here's the answer"

Because we're so used to writing the answer after "3+4=", it's easy to mistake the equals sign for a marker showing "where the answer goes." But its real meaning is "the left and right sides have the same value."

3 + 4 = 5 + 2

Both sides equal 7, so this equation is true. The right side isn't "the answer" — it's just another way of writing the same value.

2Thinking of a balance scale makes it click

Put the same weight on both sides of a balance scale, and it settles level. The equals sign works the same way — when both sides of an equation have the same value, they're "balanced," and when they don't, the scale tips to one side.

Both sides equal 7, so the scale balances — the equals sign describes exactly this balanced state

3□ and △ are symbols for an unknown number

Even before learning letters like x and y, you can write a rule using symbols like □ and △. "△ = □ + 3" is a rule saying: whatever number □ is, add 3 to it to get △.

When □ = 1, △ = 4. When □ = 2, △ = 5.

Even as □ changes, the rule "add 3" always stays the same.

4Knowing the rule lets you predict future values

A rule has the power to predict the future.
If you know the rule "△ = □ × 2," you can find △ just by calculating — no matter how large □ gets, without ever having to count it out by hand.

5Try playing like this

  • Find the moment the scale balances. Move the sliders and try to find several combinations where the "=" sign appears.
  • Switch rules and compare the tables. Compare how differently "□+3" and "□×2" turn the same □ value into △.
  • Try solving the true-or-false quiz in your head. Build the habit of calculating both sides yourself to check whether they're really equal.
